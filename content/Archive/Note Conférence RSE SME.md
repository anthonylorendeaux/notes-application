# Key definitions

## Social responsibility

An organization has a responsibility for the impacts of its decisions and activities on society, the environment, and ethical matters. To fulfill this responsibility, the organization should:

- Contribute to sustainable development
- Take into account the expectations of stakeholders
- Be in compliance with the law and consistent with international norms of behavior
- Integrate this responsibility throughout the organization and practice it in its relationships

## Sustainable development

Sustainable development involves meeting the needs of society while living within the planet's ecological limits and without jeopardizing the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

## Increasing social responsibility

This contributes to a virtuous cycle in which each action strengthens the organization and the community, promoting sustainable development.


# Integrate social responsibility into organization

## ISO 26000 : Management of integration

- ISO 26000 provides guidance on social responsibility
- It was published in 2010
- Unlike other ISO standards, this guidance cannot be certified as a requirement
- It helps clarify the concept of social responsibility and provides actionable ideas for implementation
- It also includes best practices.

## What makes ISO 26000 important and credible

- It is designed to work in all organizational and cultural contexts, in any country or region.
- It is flexible, and the user decides how to use it.
- It was internationally negotiated through the ISO's consensus method.
- It incorporates the real-life experiences of its many contributors.

## ISO 26000 : New way of management

- Governance
- 7 principles

## ISO 26000 : Governance

- Governance refers to the way rules, norms, and actions are structured, sustained, regulated, and held accountable.
- The degree of formality depends on the internal rules of a given organization, as well as its external relationships with business partners.

## ISO 26000 : 7 principles

1. Accountability
2. Transparency
3. Ethical behaviour
4. Respect for the stakeholders
5. Respect for the rule of law
6. Respect for international norms of behavior
7. Respect for human rights

## ISO 26000 : Accountability and Transparency

- Accountability is the state of being answerable for decisions and activities to the organization's governing bodies, legal authorities, and, more broadly, its stakeholders.
- Transparency means being open about decisions and activities that affect society, the economy, and the environment. It requires a willingness to communicate these in a clear, accurate, timely, honest, and complete manner.
- Accountability and transparency entail taking responsibility for decisions and policies. They involve both top decision-makers and all individuals throughout a chain of command.

## ISO 26000 : Principle of Ethical Behaviour

- Ethical behavior involves deciding what the right course of action is on a day-to-day basis.
- Ethical behavior is defined as "behavior that is in accordance with accepted principles of right or good conduct in the context of a particular situation…". Ask yourself: would you be comfortable if your actions became public knowledge?

## ISO 26000 : Principle of Respect for stakeholder interests

- This involves identifying groups of stakeholders, those who are affected by your decisions and actions, and responding to their concerns.
- It does not mean letting them make your decisions.

## ISO 26000 : Principle of Respect for the rule of law

In the context of social responsibility, respect for the rule of law is an essential factor that contributes to the sustainability of an organization. This means that the organization must comply with all applicable laws and regulations, regardless of whether or not they are adequately enforced. The importance of this cannot be overstated, as it not only ensures the safety and well-being of the organization's employees and stakeholders, but also helps to build trust with the surrounding community. Additionally, adherence to the rule of law can have a positive impact on the organization's bottom line, as it reduces the risk of legal action and reputational damage. Therefore, it is crucial for organizations to prioritize respect for the rule of law in their operations and decision-making processes.

## ISO 26000 : Respect for international norms of behaviour

- When the law or its implementation does not provide adequate environmental or social safeguards, an organization should strive to respect, at a minimum, international norms of behavior.
- International norms of behavior are derived from customary international law, generally accepted principles of international law, or intergovernmental agreements that are universally or nearly universally recognized.
- Authoritative international instruments from organizations such as the United Nations and the International Labour Organization (ILO) contain these norms.

## ISO 26000 : Principle of respect for human rights

- ISO 26000 encourages its users to identify vulnerable populations among their stakeholders and work to ensure their fair treatment.
- In situations where human rights are not protected, take steps to respect human rights and avoid taking advantage of these situations.

## ISO 26000 : Summary the 7 Principles

- Establish the foundation for socially responsible decision-making.
- Connect each utilization of ISO 26000 to a global community that shares its principles.
- Emphasize that social responsibility is an evolving process that develops through practice.

## ISO 26000 : The 7 Core Subjects


- Each of the 7 core subjects is relevant to every organization and should be considered.
- Users then review specific issues listed under each core subject to identify those issues that are relevant and significant.


## Stakeholder engagement and communication: a crucial component

1. Effective stakeholder identification and engagement play a crucial role in fulfilling an organization's social responsibility.
2. Communication acts as a vital conduit for exchanging knowledge, suggestions, complaints, and ideas, which are necessary for finding solutions to problems.
3. One of the most rewarding yet challenging aspects of fulfilling social responsibility is identifying stakeholders and establishing communication channels with them.
4. It is essential to start communicating respect and willingness to engage with stakeholders well in advance of a crisis.
5. The ultimate goal is to establish trust and credibility with stakeholders for the long term, rather than finding quick fixes to address problems.

## Who are your stakeholders

- Stakeholders are individuals or groups who are impacted by the actions of your organization.
- Additionally, stakeholders may also have the power to affect your organization in return.
  - This is precisely why ISO 26000 emphasizes the importance of stakeholder involvement and provides suggestions on how to go about it.
- The categories of stakeholders can include workers, clients, purchasers, consumers, owners, investors, government officials, community residents, and suppliers.
  - For instance, in the context of a hospital, stakeholders could comprise of doctors, nurses, patients, patients' families, hospital owners (who could be a branch of government or private investors), the community where the hospital is situated, as well as suppliers of medical items.

## Stakeholders engagement

- It is crucial to involve different stakeholder groups, including the community, workers, and other stakeholders, through leader engagement. Additionally, it is essential to seek participation from the broader population to ensure fairness and gain diverse viewpoints.
- Emphasizing two-way communication is vital in engaging stakeholders. This means listening to your stakeholders' perspectives while also explaining your organization's position. It is essential to maintain a realistic and positive tone and avoid making vague or overly ambitious promises that cannot be kept.
- Communication should not be used mainly as a tool for publicity or photo opportunities, but rather as a genuine way of engaging with stakeholders and building trust.

- Setting direction toward SR
  - Owners and top management need to lead
  - Use mission and vision statements to define values
  - Involve relevant stakeholders, including those working for the organisation
  - Set short-term and long-term goals
- Governance and operating procedures
  - Incorporate transparency and accountability at all levels
  - Apply SR to decisions on purchasing, investing, hiring, and promoting, advertising, community relations, etc.

## CSR Theory

- The Corporations are members of the moral community and are viewed as citizens of the world
- They have responsibilities like other members of the moral community
- 4 types of responsibility :
  - Economic : Responsibility of a business to take money = human survival instinct. If they don't make money, they "die"
  - Legal : Responsibility to obey the letter and the spirit of the law
  - Ethical : Responsibility to do the right thing when neither the spirit nor the letter of the law applies
  - Philanthropic : Responsibility to contributes to society even when they are independent of the company

# Environmental management system

- Environmental management is a part of a sustainable development policy
- It refers to the management methods of a company
- It is used to understand the environmental impacts of it's activities so as to evaluate and reduce them.
- How do you structure an EMS ?
  - ISO 14001 makes it possible to comprehend the approach, but it must be adapted / adjusted according to the company.
  - Environmental certification procedures (CERWAY HQE approach, DGNB, LEED, BREEAM).
- Environmental management system describes in a managerial way, the commitment of the company regarding environmental issues within it's projects.

## A management tool

- Why ?
  - To reduce and control it's environmental impacts
  - Respect the regulations
  - Better marketing image of the company (new markets, customer relations, etc.)
  - To rethink the company's strategy through the environment (resource allocation, human resources, R&D).
  - Aim for an environmental certification for the company
- How ?
  - By answering the following questions:
    - What is our environmental strategy ?
    - Who does what ?
    - How are we going to execute this strategy ?

## General structure


## Context of Organization §4

- Definitions
- Organization : institutions, charities and even the self-employed are organizations.
- Issues : Anything that can flow from the legal, competitive, cultural, social, economic, external and internal environment of the organization.
- Stakeholders : Not limited to the customer. We must also consider the environment, suppliers, transporters, bankers, regulations and even the standard on the management system. The needs and expectations of the interested parties are grouped together as requirements.

## Leadership §5

- Definitions
- Management : person, at the highest level of the organization, who directs and controls the activities of the organization, who directs and controls the activities of the organization. Some of the authority can be delegated (to the heads of services, the quality manager...).
- Resources : human but also material, natural, financial…
- Politics : expression to the intentions and orientations of the organization, in the form of commitments.

## Planning §6

- Definitions
- Objective : the result to be achieved.
- Risk : is characterized by it's probability of occurrence, it's origin, it's consequences.

## Support §7

- Support for the organization's activities resolves around :
  - Resources
  - Information
  - Communication
- The resources are multiple
- In the case of human resources skills must be defined and proven
  - The article emphasizes the awareness of the staff, who must feel involved.

## Operation §8

- The organization divides its activities into internal and external processes, which are the basis for its indicators.
- The standards provide detailed information on operations, where many requirements must be met in order to achieve operational activities.
- ISO 14001:2015 lays down requirements for determining and reviewing requirements, purchasing, design and development, production, delivery, and services.

## Evaluation of performances §9

- The organization defines the activities of monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation (what, when, how).
- Internal audits are conducted to gain a good understanding of the management system (compliance with requirements, efficiency, etc.).
- Management reviews consider the organization's challenges, performance information, ongoing actions, and other relevant factors.

## Improvement §10

- The organization is committed to a dynamic of continuous improvement.
- In the event of non-compliance, the organization must take control of the situation, correct it, and address any consequences.
- If the causes of non-compliance could recur, the organization must take corrective action to eliminate them. Such action may have a potential impact on the Environmental Management System.

## Guidelines and actions

- Conduct an environmental review of the company's business operations.
- Consider the environmental impact of product design and corporate services.
- Create an action plan to reduce pollution.
- Set limits on the use of non-renewable resources.
- Set limits on energy consumption.
- Develop a training plan and provide information on environmental actions.
- Comply with one or more environmental standards (HQE, DGNB, BREEAM).

## Implementation methodology

- Management brainstorming/discussion
- Internal audit and list of impacts for the company
- External audit of the customer's environmental expectations
- Determination of priority actions
- Determination of the validation processes of priority actions
- Establishment of indicators to follow the achievement or priority actions
- Implementation of priority actions
- Implementation of the indicators
